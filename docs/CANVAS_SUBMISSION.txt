Implemented connections (using the supplied starter pages and one additional page):

1. Phone is shaken -> Bells.
   The interaction layer detects two alternating acceleration peaks in a short window and opens the English church-bell gate; the starter `deviceShaken()` callback remains only as a fallback. The action resembles shaking a handbell, giving a clear physical metaphor for rhythmic interaction.

2. Phone detects very large movements, but not small ones -> Thunder.
   The starter p5 movement threshold is used to ignore small movements; `deviceMoved()` triggers the thunder rumble for a strong gesture. A forceful gesture fits the expressive intensity of a virtual storm.

3. Phone is placed flat in the hand and tilted from side to side -> Brass.
   The phone's roll angle is continuously mapped to the Faust brass blower pressure in the range 0–1. More tilt therefore produces more air pressure and a stronger brass sound.

4. Phone is used to point straight up -> Sci-Fi photon torpedo.
   The signed pitch angle must remain above 65 degrees for 350 ms before one torpedo is triggered. Pointing down has the opposite sign and is ignored; lowering the phone rearms the gesture.

All four sounds use Faust synthesis models compiled to WebAssembly and the course `dsp.js` bridge. Physical-phone verification and laboratory-assistant approval have not yet been recorded; desktop mouse rehearsal is only a debugging aid.
